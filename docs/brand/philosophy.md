# Kramer v AI: Philosophy

*Phase 1 of the brand-skill. The strategic essence, built on the emotive narrative.*

## Core Concept

The central metaphor is **the overlap**: the small patch of shared ground where two irreconcilable accounts of a marriage can both stand. Two people, two whole and incompatible truths, and one narrow region they can each live inside. Everything the brand is flows from that region. We are not a louder advocate or a cheaper lawyer. We are the thing that finds the ground and helps two people stand on it.

This makes the brand's posture unusual for legal-tech: it does not take a side, and it does not promise a win. Its archetype is the **Sage** (neutral clarity, seeing what neither party can see alone) carried by the **Caregiver** (protection, holding people through their worst). Wise and kind at once. The register is grave, because this is grief work, not a productivity gain.

The name carries the idea. "Kramer **v** AI" reads as *versus*, the adversarial marker the whole product exists to undo. The brand inverts it: the name says *versus*, but everything we do says *together*. Hold that tension and the brand explains itself. (How that's expressed visually is a design question, not a philosophy one.)

## Voice

How Kramer v AI communicates:

**Grave, not grim.** It speaks to people on a hard day and respects the weight of it. It doesn't cheer, doesn't sell, doesn't reach for optimism that hasn't been earned. Calm, steady, unhurried.

**Plain over clever.** Short sentences. Ordinary words. It would rather be clearly understood than admired. No jargon, no flourish, nothing that performs intelligence at the reader's expense.

**Humane, never clinical.** It names feelings and facts directly but gently. It never reduces a person to a label, a score, or a data point. Warmth is the default; precision appears only where it earns trust.

### Examples

**On-brand:**
- "You'll both be heard separately, in confidence. Nothing you say here is shown to the other side."
- "This is the range a court would likely accept. It isn't a verdict, and it isn't advice — it's the ground you can both stand on."

**Off-brand:**
- "Win your divorce with AI." / "The smartest way to beat your ex in court."
- "Our algorithm has determined your emotional profile with 82% confidence."
- "Revolutionising the legal landscape." (hype, jargon)

## Principles

1. **Two truths, never one** — We don't adjudicate who's right; there's no such answer to find. We hold both accounts as valid and look for where they meet.

2. **Preserve the *Thou*** — The other person is never an opponent or an asset to be won. The moment we frame them as a target, we've become the thing we replaced.

3. **Safety before progress** — If there is fear or coercion in the room, the machine stops and a human steps in. Nothing proceeds over the top of that.

4. **Durable over decisive** — We are judged on whether a settlement lasts, not on whether it was clever or fast to reach. A result that doesn't hold is a failure, however elegant.

5. **Plain over clever** — Clarity is a form of respect. We choose the plain word, the quiet design, the honest number over the impressive one.

## The Feeling

What people should feel with Kramer v AI:

1. **Heard** — Listened to fully, on their own terms, by something that isn't keeping score.

2. **Held** — Safe enough to trust it with the worst week of their life; the weight is shared, not dumped back on them.

3. **Relief and dignity** — The sense that the war can actually end, and that they can come out of it as someone they still recognise.

## Language

**Use:**
- "shared ground", "the overlap" — the core idea
- "a settlement that holds" — durability over victory
- "heard", "in confidence", "on your own terms" — the intake promise
- "open offer", "a court would likely accept" — precise, honest framing
- "end the war" — what we're actually doing

**Avoid:**
- "win", "beat", "opponent", "your case", "fight" — adversarial register
- "revolutionary", "disrupt", "game-changing" — startup hype
- "subject", "profile", "diagnosis", confidence scores — clinical, in any user-facing copy
- "deal", "close" — transactional coldness
